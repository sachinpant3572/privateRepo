## 1. The planned city

### 1.1 Washington DC

- **L'Enfant Plan (1791)**: diagonal avenues radiating from the Capitol and the President's House,
  laid over a grid of residential streets. Where the diagonals crossed the grid he marked 15
  "reservations" as public open space. [V: NCPC/NPS history, TCLF, Senate.gov]
- **Widths**: Pennsylvania and Maryland Avenues, the widest, at 160 ft (48.8 m) [S]. The planned
  "Grand Avenue" (the future Mall) was 400 ft (122 m) wide and about a mile long [S: NPS vistas].
- Ellicott's 1792 engraving straightened or dropped some radials [S].
- **Circles** (Dupont, Logan, Washington) frame monuments and green space; Washington Circle was
  Reservation No. 26. [V: Library of Congress, TCLF]
- **McMillan Plan (1901-02)**, the Senate Park Commission: restored the cluttered Mall; Union
  Station (1907) replaced a rail station that stood on it. [V]
- **The Mall today**: about 2 mi (3.2 km) from the Capitol to the Lincoln Memorial. [V: National
  Mall Coalition, NGA]
- Federal Triangle (1920s) erased 23 of L'Enfant's blocks [S].
- **Height Act (1910)**: on commercial streets, height = street width + 20 ft, never above 130 ft
  (39.6 m); 160 ft (48.8 m) on the north side of Pennsylvania Ave, 1st-15th St NW; on residential
  streets, width - 10 ft, never above 90 ft (27.4 m). [V: NCPC, Wikipedia, DowntownDC] Result: a
  flat skyline that the domes and the obelisk rise above.
- Washington Monument at 555 ft (169 m): [?].

### 1.2 Manhattan (Commissioners' Plan, 1811)

- **Street widths**: avenues 100 ft (30.5 m); cross streets 60 ft (18.3 m); 15 major cross streets
  (14th to 155th) 100 ft. [V: MCNY "Greatest Grid", StreetEasy]
- **Block depth (north-south)**: 200 ft (61 m). Block plus street is about 260 ft, so about 20
  blocks per mile. [V: MCNY, StreetEasy, Baruch] Real blocks run 181-206 ft (1892 survey) [S].
- **Block length (east-west)** depends on position: 1st-2nd Ave 650 ft (198 m); 2nd-3rd 610 ft
  (186 m); 3rd-6th 920 ft (280 m); 6th-12th 800 ft (244 m); average about 750 ft (229 m).
  [V: MCNY, StreetEasy, Baruch] So blocks are 3 to 4.5 times longer than deep.
- The plan set no lot sizes (the 25 x 100 ft lot came later) and covered about 1,600 blocks.
  [S: Gotham Center]
- **Broadway**: the older road running diagonally across the grid, leaving triangle squares where
  it crosses avenues. [I: from plan maps]
- **Central Park**: 843 acres (341 ha), about 2.5 x 0.5 mi (4.0 x 0.8 km), Olmsted and Vaux (won
  the 1858 competition); first 59th-106th St, later extended to 110th. [V: Wikipedia, SHU blog]

### 1.3 San Francisco

- **Two grids**: O'Farrell's 1847 survey swung Vioget's 1839 grid by about 2.5° and set Market
  Street, parallel to Mission, as the seam between a small-block grid to the north and a larger,
  differently angled grid to the south. [V: Wikipedia, LocalWiki, sfgenealogy]
- South-of-Market blocks 600 x 400 ft (183 x 122 m), 100-vara lots against 50-vara to the north
  [S; sources disagree on 2x or 4x lot size]. Market Street's width (often 120 ft): [?].
- The grid runs straight over steep hills, which is why cable cars exist. [I; no gradient sourced]
- **Cable cars**: first run Aug 2, 1873 on Clay St (Hallidie); National Historic Landmark 1964;
  the world's last manually operated system; three lines: Powell-Hyde 2.1 mi, Powell-Mason 1.6 mi,
  California St 1.4 mi. [V: SFMTA, Wikipedia]
- **Golden Gate Bridge**: opened May 1937; main span 4,200 ft (1,280 m), about 1.7 mi long;
  "International Orange"; then the world's longest and tallest suspension bridge. [V: Structurae,
  EBSCO, Encyclopedia.com]
- **Painted Ladies**: Victorians at 710-720 Steiner St facing Alamo Square ("Postcard Row"), built
  1892-96. [V: Wikipedia, In Your Pocket]
- **Embarcadero**: the double-deck freeway (late 1950s), damaged in the 1989 Loma Prieta quake, was
  torn down in 1991, reopening the waterfront and Ferry Building; a plaza replaced it. [V: SPUR,
  CNU, MTC]

### 1.6 What a "best planned" capital-like city should blend [I]

1. **DC radial skeleton**: 3-5 diagonal avenues (about 1.6x a normal avenue's width) meeting at a
   capitol landmark, with circles where they cross the grid. The star reads on the minimap at once,
   the opposite of the "chessboard" problem in `reference/MAP_BUILDING.md`.
2. **A Mall axis**: one open green strip 6-10 blocks long and 2-3 wide, from the capitol to a
   memorial, lined with museums. It is the main Lynch path and landmark, clearest at 3/4 view.
3. **Manhattan-proportion blocks in the core**: about 1 : 3.5 (real: 61 x 186-280 m), for example
   40 x 140 m at game scale. Narrow streets (18 m equivalent) against wide avenues (30 m) so the two
   look different. One **Broadway-style diagonal** that cuts the grid and leaves triangle plazas.
4. **One big rectangular park** (Central Park ratio about 5 : 1) as a district edge and chase space.
5. **A Market Street seam**: one grand angled boulevard where two grid orientations meet, so the
   change of angle announces a new district. SF-style hills with cable cars suit one "heights"
   district with views.
6. **A Height Act cap**: an even 6-10 storey skyline except the dome, an obelisk and the station.
   Tall towers hide streets and the player from a top-down camera; a low skyline keeps streets
   readable and lets the landmarks stand out.
7. **An Embarcadero waterfront**: promenade, ferry building and piers, a hard edge for the map.

Why it reads well from above: radials and circles are nodes, the Mall and park are districts and
edges, and a low skyline with a few tall landmarks gives orientation (Lynch).

### 2.2 Vacant land and abandonment

- **Motor City Mapping (2014)** surveyed about 380,000 parcels: 84,641 blighted structures or vacant
  lots needing work (about 22%), 40,077 recommended for immediate demolition (about 10.5%), 6,135+
  lots with dumping; fix estimated at $850 M. [V: Data Driven Detroit, Michigan Public, CBS]
- **Land Bank**: about 97,000 parcels in 2016; about 75,000 of 380,000 (just under 20%) more
  recently, about 85% of them vacant lots; the city's biggest landowner and the largest land bank
  in the US. Demolitions turned houses into empty lots. [V: Outlier Media, WGBH, Next City]
- **"Urban prairie"**: 100,000-120,000 vacant lots and about 18 sq mi (47 km²) of unused land
  [S: DFC podcast, a blog]. The 2012 Detroit Future City framework first mapped the whole
  vacant-land inventory [V]. The phrase "urban prairie" itself was not found in a source.
- About 40% of streetlights did not work in July 2013. [V: several outlets]

### 2.3 Street plan: the 1805 fire and the Woodward Plan

- After the 1805 fire, Judge Woodward drew a L'Enfant-inspired plan: **hexagonal blocks**, plazas
  with radiating avenues, centred on the Grand Circus; avenues alternating 200 ft (61 m) and 120 ft
  (36.6 m). [V: Wikipedia, Clio, Metro Times]
- Abandoned about 1817-18; only fragments built (half of Grand Circus, Campus Martius, the
  spokes). [V]
- **Surviving spokes**: Woodward, Michigan, Grand River, Gratiot and Jefferson Avenues and Fort
  Street. [V]
- **8 Mile Road** is the city's northern boundary with the suburbs. The **Birwood Wall** (1941): a
  6 ft (1.8 m) concrete wall running three blocks south from 8 Mile so a developer could get
  federal loans for white buyers; National Register 2021. [V: Wikipedia, Detroit PBS, Historic
  Detroit]
- Takeaway [I]: a broken star of radials around a half-circle downtown, a grid filling the rest,
  then large patches of that grid emptied. Very readable from above: whole blocks of grass with
  one house left standing.

### 2.6 Industry, unrest and crime

- **UAW**: founded in Detroit in 1935; HQ "Solidarity House" on East Jefferson from 1951, by Oskar
  Stonorov. [V: Reuther Library, UAW, JHSM]
- **1967 uprising**: from Jul 23, five days; 43 dead, over 7,200 arrests [V]. Buildings burned or
  looted: about 2,500 (NPR), "more than 1,000" (Britannica) or "more than 400 destroyed"
  (Wikipedia) [S].
- **Devil's Night**: arson on the night before Halloween, 810 fires in 1984; answered after 1994 by
  "Angels' Night" with about 50,000 volunteer patrols. [S: Refinery29 citing Atlas Obscura]
- **Bankruptcy**: filed Jul 18, 2013, about $18-20 bn of debt, the largest municipal bankruptcy in
  US history. [V]
- **Heroin era (YBI)**: formed 1976; controlled about 80% of Detroit heroin traffic from 1978; used
  juveniles as young as 12 as runners; leaders and 41 lieutenants indicted in 1982. [V: Wikipedia,
  DOJ/NCJRS, Deadline Detroit] End date disputed (1982 or 1987).
- **Crack era (Chambers Brothers)**: brothers from rural Arkansas (Lee County / Marianna) ran crack
  houses in the mid-1980s. [V: Wikipedia, UMich History Lab]
- Patterns for fictional crews [I]: teenage runners, crack houses in abandoned homes, arson for
  profit and for kicks, scrap theft from dead factories, smuggling over a private border bridge.

## 3. Las Vegas

### 3.1 Form: the Strip against Fremont Street

- **The Strip**: a 4.2 mi (6.8 km) stretch of Las Vegas Boulevard (51.4 mi in all), **outside the
  city limits** in unincorporated Paradise and Winchester. [V: Wikipedia, Nevada Independent,
  Gigafact]
- **Downtown** began as Clark's Las Vegas Townsite, a railroad town auctioned May 15, 1905 at
  Fremont and Main. Each block held 32 lots of 25 x 140 ft (7.6 x 42.7 m). **Block 16** was the
  legal vice block (liquor, red-light district). [V: city PDF, Casino.org, UNLV] Original size
  38-40 blocks [S].
- **Fremont Street Experience** (Dec 14, 1995): an LED canopy 1,375 ft (419 m) long (often rounded
  to 1,500 ft) and about 90 ft (27 m) high over five blocks of old casinos. [V: Wikipedia, Las
  Vegas Advisor]
- Takeaway [I]: one bright linear spine with resorts set back behind car parks and plazas, a small
  dense railhead grid, car-scale suburbs everywhere else.

### 3.2 Casino and resort architecture

- **Themed resorts**: Caesars Palace (1966, Rome); Circus Circus; The Mirage (1989, tropical, the
  first modern mega-resort); Excalibur (Jun 19, 1990, medieval castle); Luxor (Oct 15, 1993,
  black-glass pyramid, 30 storeys, 350 ft / 106 m); New York-New York and Paris. [V: Wikipedia, Las
  Vegas Advisor, CDC Gaming] After 2000, theming faded into "atmosphere" [S].
- ***Learning from Las Vegas*** (Venturi, Scott Brown, Izenour; from a 1968 Yale studio): the
  **decorated shed** (plain box behind a giant sign) against the **duck** (a building shaped like
  what it sells). [V: 99% Invisible, Strong Towns] Two building types for the game.
- **Neon Museum Boneyard**: about 2 acres of retired signs (2.27-acre campus); the relocated La
  Concha motel lobby is the visitor centre; founded 1996, opened to the public Oct 27, 2012. [V]

### 3.3 Mob history

- **Mob Museum** (opened Feb 14, 2012) in the 1933 federal courthouse and post office. The Kefauver
  Committee held its 7th of 27 hearings in its second-floor courtroom on Nov 15, 1950. The city
  bought the building for $1 in 2002. [V: Mob Museum, WWNO]
- **The Spilotro era**: the Chicago Outfit's enforcer came in 1971 to protect the **skim** at four
  casinos (Stardust, Fremont, Hacienda, Marina) and ran the "Hole in the Wall Gang", burglars who
  broke in through walls. He and his brother were killed in June 1986 (14th or 23rd) and found in
  an Indiana cornfield. [V: Mob Museum, Wikipedia, Biography] Use only the pattern: skimming the
  count room, an enforcer-burglar crew, a federal hearing.

### 3.4 The desert around it

- **Hoover Dam** (1936): 726.4 ft (221 m) high, 1,244 ft (379 m) long. **Lake Mead**, the largest
  US reservoir (28-29 M acre-feet), has been below full since 1983 (drought shorelines, "bathtub
  rings"). [V: Wikipedia, USBR, Roadtrip USA]
- **Red Rock Canyon NCA**: 17 mi west of the Strip; 13-mile one-way scenic drive with 12 trailheads
  [V: BLM, Recreation.gov]; 185,000-198,000 acres [S].
- **Ghost towns**: Rhyolite (boom about 1905-07, peak 4,000-10,000 people, a Bottle House of about
  50,000 bottles, Goldwell sculptures) [S]; Goldfield (about 20,000 people in 1906, briefly
  Nevada's largest city; 225 in 2020) [V: Wikipedia, UNLV]; Nelson / Eldorado Canyon (Techatticup
  Mine, flood-wrecked in 1974, a filming location) [S]. International Car Forest: [?].
- **Nevada Test and Training Range (Nellis)**: 2.9 M acres (about 11,700 km²) of land plus 12,000
  sq mi (31,000 km²) of restricted airspace [V: Nellis AFB captions, WorldAtlas, Review-Journal];
  a proposed expansion of about 300,000 acres [S].
- **Area 51**: inside the NTTR at Groom Lake; main runway 3,657 m (11,998 ft) [S: Wikipedia]; its
  own area [?].
- **Creech AFB** (Indian Springs): drone training and the Joint Unmanned Aerial Systems Center of
  Excellence; an emergency divert field for the NTTR; Thunderbirds training site. [S: Wikipedia]
- **Biggest range?** Listicles call White Sands Missile Range (about 3,200 sq mi / 2.05 M acres)
  the largest US installation [S, low quality]. By the Air Force's 2.9 M acres (about 4,500 sq mi),

## 4. Florida (Miami-led)

### 4.1 Miami districts

- **Miami Beach Architectural District (Art Deco District)**: listed May 14, 1979, the first urban
  20th-century historic district in the US; about 800 contributing buildings (Wikipedia: 960); Art
  Deco, Mediterranean Revival, Streamline Moderne and MiMo; Ocean Drive's hotels (5th-15th St) face
  Lummus Park and the beach. [V: Wikipedia, MDPL, county tourism site]
- **Little Havana**: west of downtown, the historic heart of Cuban Miami, along Calle Ocho (SW 8th
  St) and Flagler; **Máximo Gómez "Domino" Park** at 8th St and 15th Ave has about 10 covered
  tables; now mixed Latin American. [V: Wikipedia, TCLF, county tourism site]
- **Wynwood**: warehouse district; Tony Goldman began the Wynwood Walls in 2009 with six buildings
  on 25th-26th St as mural canvases ("the world's largest outdoor street-art museum"). [V:
  Wikipedia, Bisnow, the Walls' site]
- **Brickell / Downtown**: high-rise finance core on the free Metromover; skyline facts [?].
- **PortMiami**: 8,233,056 cruise passengers in FY2024 (+12.8%), the world's busiest cruise port.
  [V: Seatrade, KTVZ/CNN, Hoodline]

### 4.2 Beyond Miami

- **Everglades National Park**: about 1.5 M acres (6,100 km²), dedicated 1947, UNESCO World
  Heritage; "River of Grass" (Douglas), Miccosukee name Pa-hay-okee ("grassy water"); Miccosukee
  businesses and airboat tours along the Tamiami Trail (US 41); Big Cypress to the west. [V; one
  outlier gives 2 M acres]
- **The Keys**: the Overseas Highway, 113 mi (182 km) with 42 bridges [S; Wikipedia infobox 106.5
  mi], reuses Flagler's Over-Sea Railroad (to Key West 1912), wrecked by the Category 5 **Labor Day

### 5.5 Showing transit condition in gameplay [I]

- **Excellent (planned city)**: trains every 1-2 minutes, always on time, so the metro is cheap
  fast travel. Clean stations with cameras and transit police (more heat for crime underground).
  Cable cars you can ride on the outside (a chase set piece). Union station as the intercity hub.
- **Decaying (Detroit-like)**: buses late or missing ("next bus: 40 min" boards, breakdowns
  mid-route); the empty elevated loop is a scenic but useless ride, good for an ambush or a quiet
  hideout; boarded stations become crew hideouts or dungeons; the dead city airport is a smuggling
  strip; the one new streetcar is the safe "comeback corridor". Little fast travel: a car or bike
  is the real way around.
- **Tourist-only (Vegas-like)**: monorail and Strip bus serve only the tourist spine, with night
  surge pricing; suburbs and desert need a car; no train out until a story beat finishes the
  high-speed line (the bible's Solvera Line); helicopter tours as air travel and an activity.
- **Car-centric (Florida-like)**: roads and boats first; the free downtown loop is the one transit
  gift; the private fast train reaches the theme-park city; jams on the island highway; hurricane
  evacuations clog roads and close bridges.
