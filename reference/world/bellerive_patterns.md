# Top-down patterns from Washington DC, New York and California (for Bellerive v3)

Research for the Bellerive v3 task (2026-10-27), done through web search summaries only: map,
tile and encyclopedia hosts could not be opened from the container. [V] means a named source
states the fact; [I] means inference or memory. Decisions that use this live in
`HANDOFF-bellerive-v3.md`; the earlier Concordia research is in `real_cities.md` section 1.

The researcher ran about 45 web searches.

- **Tags:** [V] means a source states the fact and is named beside it. [I] means it is my own inference or memory.
- **Source quality:** many results were Wikipedia mirrors, real-estate listings or blogs. Where a figure rests on a weak source, I say so.
- **Units:** 1 ft = 0.3048 m and 1 acre = 4,047 m².

## 1. Washington DC

**Capitol and grounds**
- **The building:** 751 ft x 350 ft (229 x 107 m). It is 288 ft (88 m) from the east-front base line to the top of the statue. [V: Architect of the Capitol (AOC)]
- **Original grounds:** 58.8 acres (23.8 ha) inside a stone wall, bounded by Constitution Ave, Independence Ave and 1st St on the east and west sides. Later additions bring the total to about 274 acres. [V: AOC]
- **West terrace:** a marble terrace wraps the north, west and south sides. It runs about 1,600 ft (488 m) and rises up to 20 ft (6 m). [V: AOC]
- **East Front plaza:** Olmsted's plaza has two large rectangular fountains and big lanterns. [V: AOC]
- **Reflecting pool:** 6 acres (2.4 ha), more than half of Union Square, about 1.5 ft deep. [V: AOC, mallhistory.org] I found no length or width.
- **Approach geometry:** the radial avenues (Pennsylvania, Maryland, Delaware, New Jersey) end at the edge of the grounds and become curving drives. They do not reach the building. [I: from map memory]
- Game use: a raised dome block about 200 x 100 m on a terrace 6 m above a lawn of about 250 x 300 m. Put a hard plaza with two fountain basins on the city side and a pool of about 180 x 120 m [I] facing the Mall. Stop the diagonals at a ring drive.

**Federal Triangle**
- **Site:** a wedge between Pennsylvania and Constitution Avenues, 6th to 15th St NW. The 1973 National Register nomination calls it a 75-acre complex. [V: NRHP] A local-history page says about 10 acres, which I think counts something smaller.
- **Cornice and storeys:** the cornice line is almost uniform at about 125-130 ft (38-40 m) on 7-storey buildings. Each has a 2-storey base, a 4-storey middle and a set-back top storey. [V: NRHP nomination]
- **Materials and courts:** limestone over a granite base, with red-tile hipped or mansard roofs. The blocks are pierced by light courts; the Ariel Rios building has six. [V: GSA, NPS]
- **Mellon Auditorium group:** about 6 acres, running 1,000 ft (305 m) along Constitution. [V: GSA]
- **12th Street hemicycle:** the plan called for a circular plaza on 12th St made of two semicircular facades. [V: GSA] As far as I recall, only half was built. [I]
- Game use: a superblock about 300-450 m long, split into 3-4 linked buildings about 90-120 m deep. Give each 2-6 courtyards (red roofs around pale courts read very well from above) and one 38 m cornice line. Add one half-round plaza.

**Traffic circles**
- **Logan Circle:** the park is 360 ft (110 m) across. The curb-to-curb diameter is about 450 ft (137 m). [V: NRHP 1972]
- **Thomas Circle:** about 339 ft (103 m). Massachusetts Ave passes under it in an underpass opened in 1940. [V: Wikipedia]
- **Washington Circle:**
  - roundabout diameter 119.9 m and a park of 2.19 acres [V: Kittelson database];
  - four streets (23rd, K, New Hampshire and Pennsylvania), so 8 legs, with an equestrian statue at the centre [V];
  - K St runs in a tunnel underneath, completed in 1962 [V].
- **Dupont Circle:**
  - ten streets or legs meet it: Connecticut, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, P and 19th [V: search summary];
  - the park has two concentric ring walks, ten radial walks and a central fountain with an upper basin about 11.6 ft across [V: SAH/TCLF];
  - a streetcar underpass was finished in 1951 [V];
  - diameter is only "about a football field" [weak source].
- Game use: an outer diameter of 110-140 m and an inner park of 70-100 m. Give the park a ring path, radial paths matching the legs, and a statue or fountain at the centre. Run 4-5 streets through it (8-10 legs) and send one through-avenue under it in a cut-and-cover tunnel. The radial paths make it read like a wheel from above.

**Capitol Hill rowhouse blocks**
- **Alleys:** squares were usually laid out with a 30 ft (9 m) H-shaped or I-shaped alley, joined to the streets by narrower stubs. [V: DC Office of Planning/HPRB, CHRS]
- **Infill:** in 1880-1900 builders cut "one-block streets" through deep squares to create more rowhouse lots. Alley dwellings line the alleys. [V: CHRS]
- **Lot width:** about 16-20 ft (5-6 m) wide and about 100 ft deep is typical [I: unverified]. One zoning case gives an 18 ft minimum lot width in RF-3 zoning [V: BZA exhibit].
- Game use: blocks of about 110 x 130 m with a 9 m H-alley inside. Use rows of 5-6 m-wide houses with flat roofs and small rear yards, plus a few alley houses. The H of the alley is the signature from above.

**Union Station plaza**
- **Plaza:** a semicircular brick forecourt 940 ft x 540 ft (287 x 165 m) across the station front. [V: NPS, NRHP 1978]
- **Fountain:** Columbus Fountain is a semicircular double basin with a 45 ft (14 m) shaft. [V]
- **Flagpoles:** three poles of 110-112 ft (34 m) stand about 80 ft (24 m) north of the fountain. [V] Burnham also planned two smaller fountains at the ends.
- **Radials:** Massachusetts, Delaware and Louisiana Avenues fan out from the plaza. [I]
- Game use: a half-disc plaza about 250 x 140 m in front of the station. Put the fountain at the centre of the arc, three tall flagpoles in a row, and 2-3 avenues fanning from the arc.

## 2. New York

**Bowties (Times Square and Herald Square)**
- **Times Square:** Broadway crosses 7th Ave at about 45th St, making two long, thin triangles between 42nd and 47th Streets. [V: Snøhetta, NYC DOT] The northern one is Duffy Square (45th-47th). [V]
- **Times Square plaza:** the pedestrian plaza is 2.5 acres (1 ha). [V: Snøhetta; other listings give larger, conflicting totals]
- **Herald Square:** Broadway crosses 6th Ave at 34th St. Herald Square (34th-35th) lies to the north and Greeley Square (32nd-33rd) to the south. [V: Wikipedia, 34th Street Partnership]
- **Herald Square plazas:** closing Broadway to cars (33rd-35th, 2009) more than doubled the open space. [V]
- Game use: cross one diagonal avenue at about 20° with a grid avenue. That leaves two triangles each about 120-160 m long and 0-30 m wide. Pave them as plazas with kiosks, keeping traffic on the grid avenue only. Bright screen facades suit a 60° camera.

**Flatiron Building**
- **Lot:** 197.5 ft on Fifth Ave, 214.5 ft on Broadway, 86 ft on 22nd St (60 x 65 x 26 m). [V: Wikipedia/Britannica]
- **Tip:** 6.5 ft (2 m) wide at the point, with an apex angle of about 25°. [V]
- **Facing park:** Madison Square Park is across the street. [I: about 2.5 ha]
- Game use: a 60 x 65 x 26 m triangle tower with a 2 m nose, facing a square park. Repeat the shape wherever the diagonal splits a block.

**Columbus Circle**
- **Size:**
  - Kittelson lists a diameter of 123 m [V];
  - inner circle about 36,000 sq ft (3,300 m², about 65 m across) [V: Wikipedia mirror];
  - outer about 148,000 sq ft (13,700 m², about 132 m across) [V: Wikipedia mirror].
- **Centre:** a central column and an Olin plaza (2005) with a ring fountain. [V] The column is about 70 ft. [I]
- **Edges:** a park corner on one side and towers on the other. [I]
- Game use: a 120-130 m circle with a 65 m plaza and a monument column. Place it on the corner of the big park.

**Brooklyn brownstone blocks**
- **Lots:** 20 x 100 ft (6.1 x 30.5 m) is the standard lot in Park Slope listings. [V: many listings]
- **Houses:** about 20 x 50-55 ft (15-17 m), leaving a rear yard about 45 ft (14 m) deep. [V: listings]
- **Block interiors:** rear yards join into a green block core. One Park Slope block made it a shared yard. [V: CityLab]
- **Block size:** about 200 ft deep x 500-800 ft long. [I]
- **Promenade:**
  - 1,826 ft (557 m) long [V: Wikipedia];
  - it sits on the "triple cantilever": two expressway decks (three lanes each) over Furman St [V];
  - the halves opened in 1950 and 1951 [V].
- Game use: blocks of 60 x 180 m. Use 6 m lots with 15 m houses, a 14 m garden strip on each side and a green core about 28 m wide. Plant street trees every 8-10 m [I]. A bluff-top promenade of about 500 m can sit over a two-deck road on the waterfront edge.

**Hudson River piers**
- **Chelsea Piers:** four piers of about 900 ft (275 m). [V: 1996 CS Monitor, dated]
- **Pier 64:** about 700 ft. [V: amNY]
- **Pier 40:** a hollow square of 14.5 acres (5.9 ha) [V: Wikipedia] with a courtyard of about 4.5 acres now used as sports fields [V: Manhattan Community Board 1].
- **Pierhead line:** an Army-set pierhead line caps pier length. [V]
- **Width and spacing:** finger piers about 30-60 m wide, spaced about 100-150 m apart. [I]
- Game use: finger piers 200-275 m long and 35-50 m wide with full-length sheds, spaced 120 m apart. Add one 240 m square pier with a green field in its middle; that one reads instantly from above.

**Levittown, Long Island**
- **Lots and houses:** standard lot 60 x 100 ft (18 x 30 m). [V: listings] About 17,500 houses; the first 4,000 were four-room Cape Cods with an unfinished attic. [V]
- **Village greens and pools:** seven village greens with shops and playgrounds, and nine pools. [V: SAGE encyclopedia, Fox]
- **Streets:** curving streets. The Pennsylvania Levittown avoided four-way crossings, using T junctions inside large blocks with no houses facing the parkways. [V: weaker sources]
- **House footprint:** a Cape of about 32 x 25 ft (about 10 x 7.5 m). [I: unverified]
- Game use: 18 x 30 m lots with 10 x 8 m gabled houses set about 8 m back. Use gently curving lanes with T junctions only. Every 500-700 m, add a green with a small shop row, a pool and a playground.

**NYC civic center**
- **City Hall Park:** a triangle of about 8.8 acres (3.6 ha) between Broadway, Park Row and Chambers St. [V: NYC Landmarks Preservation Commission]
- **New York County Courthouse:** granite-faced and hexagonal, with a dominant columned portico facing Foley Square. [V: NY courts history] The Corinthian order is unconfirmed.
- **Federal courthouse:** a 37-storey, about 179 m tower on a classical base, by Cass Gilbert (1936). [V]
- **Municipal Building:** sources conflict. One says 23 storeys. [V] I recall 40 storeys and about 177 m, straddling Chambers St. [I]
- **1 Police Plaza:** a 13-storey brutalist block from 1973 on its own plaza. [V]
- Game use: a triangular civic park of about 3.5 ha with a cupola hall in the middle. Ring it with a hexagon-plan courthouse (unique from above), a tower courthouse, a brutalist police cube on a brick plaza, and a Municipal-style arch building.

## 3. California

**Los Angeles arterial grid**
- **San Fernando Valley:** main boulevards run at half-mile (805 m) intervals; there are 31 in a row from Fallbrook to Cahuenga. [V: garbell.com, informal]
- **Planning rule:** principal arterials every 1 mile and minor arterials every half mile. [V: Riverside County plan]
- **Lakewood post-war tracts:** about 5,000 sq ft lots (50 x 100 ft, 15 x 30 m). [V: KCRW, listings]
- **Between the arterials:** long residential blocks run parallel to one arterial, with few through-streets, giving a "superblock". Later tracts use loops and cul-de-sacs. [I]
- Game use: suburban arterials at 400-800 m (scaled down) and 4-6 lanes. Fill between them with 15 x 30 m lots on long blocks and a few cul-de-sacs, with commercial frontage along the arterials only.

**Downtown LA tilt**
- **Angle:** the old grid is skewed about 36° from north. The Laws of the Indies intended 45°, but the river forced 36°. [V: KCET/PBS SoCal]
- **Where it changes:** the tilt changes to the north-south survey grid west of Hoover St. [V]
- **Blocks:** "about 300 ft" [weak]. I recall about 100 x 180 m. [I]
- Game use: tilt the downtown grid about 36° against the suburban grid. The seam is an alternative to the Market Street seam.

**Beverly Hills**
- **Lots:** typical flats lots are about 50 x 123 ft (15 x 37.5 m). [V: real-estate blog citing a city survey] New subdivision lots need a depth of at least 150 ft. [V: city code]
- **Trees:** the 1906 Green and Cook plan gave each street one tree species. [V] Canary Island date palms (1910-1930) were largely replaced by Mexican fan palms around 1950 after disease. Some streets alternated the two. [V: blogs, LAist]
- **Street pattern:** a straight grid south of Sunset; curving streets in the hills north of it. [I]
- Game use: a "flats" grid of 15 x 37 m lots with deep 10 m setbacks. Give each street one tree type, with palms every 10-12 m [I] on the showcase streets. Curving lanes on the hill side.

**Strip malls and big-box centres**
- **Mini-malls:** the first opened in Panorama City in 1973: L-shaped, with parking in front. More than 600 followed, often on corner lots freed by closed gas stations. LA passed an anti-mini-mall ordinance in 1988. [V: Wikipedia, Metropolis, UCLA]
- **Strip mall size:** 5,000-100,000+ sq ft; one example corner mini-mall is 20,000 sq ft. [V]
- **Power centres:** 250,000-600,000 sq ft of leasable floor area with three or more anchors. Examples: 363,000 sq ft on 33 acres, and 140,000 sq ft on 12.9 acres. They park 4-6 spaces per 1,000 sq ft. [V: Wikipedia, Artis]
- Game use: a corner mini-mall on a 40 x 45 m lot with an L building 12 m deep and a 2-row lot. A big-box centre of 250 x 300 m: boxes along the back, a sea of parking in front, outparcels on the arterial.

**Freeway interchanges**
- **Spacing:** Caltrans sets a minimum of 1 mile in urban areas and 2 miles in rural areas. [V: Caltrans HDM, AASHTO via FHWA]
- **Relative size:** the diamond is smallest; the cloverleaf takes the most land; the stack is compact for its capacity. [V: WSDOT, others] A diamond takes about 15-25 acres of right of way [weak].
- **Footprints:** cloverleaf about 400-500 m square with loops of about 50-70 m radius; stack about 500-700 m across. [I]
- Game use: one freeway with diamonds every 800-1,600 m and a single stack where it meets a second freeway.

**Santa Monica Pier and Venice canals**
- **Pier:** 1,651.5 ft (503 m) long and up to 301 ft (92 m) wide; about 7 acres. [V: Wikipedia, Pacific Park]
- **Amusement park:** 2 acres (0.8 ha), with an 85 ft wheel. [V]
- **Venice canals:**
  - surviving canals are about 30 ft (9 m) wide and about three blocks long [V: American Canal Society];
  - the Grand Canal was 70 ft (21 m) [V];
  - 13 canals were reduced to 6 [V].
- **Venice blocks:** narrow blocks of about 40-50 m between canals, with walk-street houses. [I]
- Game use: a 300-500 m pier with a 0.8 ha amusement deck at its root. Add a grid of 9 m canals about 50 m apart with footbridges.

**San Francisco Civic Center**
- **City Hall:** dome 307 ft (94 m) [V]. It fills the 400 block of Van Ness. [V]
- **Plaza:** Civic Center Plaza covers 4.53 acres (1.8 ha) over two blocks. [V]
- **Fulton axis:** runs from Market St to the dome, flanked by the old library (now Asian Art Museum) and the new library, with Pioneer Monument in the middle. [V]
- **Van Ness twins:** the Opera House and Veterans Building (1932) face each other across Memorial Court. [V]
- Game use: a dome hall about 120 x 90 m [I] at the end of a two-block axis plaza. Flank it with twin library blocks, and put twin cultural buildings across a court behind.

**Ferry Building and Embarcadero**
- **Ferry Building:** 660 ft (201 m) long, with a 245 ft (75 m) tower and 22 ft clock dials. [V: APA, Wikipedia]
- **Piers:** odd numbers north, even south. [V] Neoclassical bulkhead buildings line the north and Mediterranean ones the south. [V: blog]
- Game use: a 200 m terminal with a 70 m tower at the foot of the main boulevard. Arched bulkhead fronts along the promenade with finger piers behind.

**Alamo Square and the Painted Ladies**
- **Park:** a 12.7-acre (5.1 ha) hilltop square park. [V]
- **Painted Ladies:** a row of six or seven Victorians on Steiner St. [V] Lots of about 25 ft (7.6 m). [I]
- Game use: a 200 x 250 m hill park with a row of 6 narrow, colourful gabled houses facing it.

**Irvine**
- **Villages:** separated by six-lane arterials, with low-traffic streets inside. [V]
- **Woodbridge:** encircled by Yale Loop, a ring road, with two lakes splitting it into four quadrants. [V]
- **University Park:** rectangular cul-de-sacs with green centres; later cul-de-sac bulbs open onto greenbelt paths. [V]
- **Arterial spacing:** about 1 mile. [I]
- Game use: a village cell of about 800 x 800 m inside the arterials, with a ring loop, cul-de-sacs off it and a central greenbelt plus lake.

**At a 60° camera**, these features read best:
- domes, which show as circles from above and as silhouettes at an angle;
- colonnades, which throw shadow stripes;
- courtyards;
- rows of flagpoles;
- half-round and square plazas;
- red-tile roofs around pale courts.

Plain slab towers read worst unless they sit on a plaza.

## The 12 strongest top-down patterns, ranked

1. Capitol dome on a terraced lawn with a long Mall axis.
2. Radial avenues with traffic circles that have ring and radial paths and a statue in the middle.
3. A diagonal avenue cutting the grid, leaving bowtie triangle plazas and a Flatiron wedge.
4. Big rectangular park against a dense grid, with Columbus Circle at its corner.
5. Finger piers on the waterfront, plus one hollow-square pier with a green field.
6. Half-moon station plaza with a fountain, flagpoles and fanned avenues.
7. Courtyard superblocks with an even cornice and red roofs (Federal Triangle).
8. A long pier with an amusement deck and wheel, over a wide beach.
9. A tilted downtown grid meeting a north-south grid along a seam boulevard.
10. Freeway stack and cloverleaf, and diamonds at regular spacing.
11. Rowhouse and brownstone blocks with green cores or H-alleys.
12. Curving suburbs and cul-de-sacs (Levittown, Irvine), and strip malls with parking in front.
